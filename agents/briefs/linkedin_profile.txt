About
Ph.D. in experimental atomic and molecular collision at ISMO, France. I analyzed Terra-bytes of experimental data from a Tandem accelerator and its silicon particle detectors.
The data is reduced to a multidimensional data set where we use a homemade 2d visualizing software "DP2" written in C and Fortran programming languages. The information from DP2 is then tabulated and analyzed using the mathematical analysis and plotting software Sigmaplot. I identified several important physical quantities like Cross section, Fragmentation branching ratio, relative probabilities of different processes that would take place in the collision.
My toolset apart from experiments with a tandem electrostatic accelerator (ALTO, Orsay) includes data reduction algorithms, noise cancellation methods, regression, and fitting.
I did a theoretical simulation of the collision experiment involves Fortran 90 and 77 programming after which we could cross check with our experiment and predict the different probabilities of systems that are difficult or near impossible to analyze experimentally.
I use Fortran to run my simulations and C++ for data reduction and analysis.
