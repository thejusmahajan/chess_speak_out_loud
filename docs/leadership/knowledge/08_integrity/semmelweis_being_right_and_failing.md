# Semmelweis — being right is not the job

**Vienna General Hospital, 1846–1850. Ignaz Semmelweis, Hungarian obstetrician.**

## The situation

The hospital had two maternity clinics. The First Clinic, staffed by doctors and medical students,
had a maternal mortality from puerperal fever of roughly 10% in bad periods. The Second Clinic,
staffed by midwives, ran at around 4%. The difference was public knowledge — women begged to be
admitted to the second, and some preferred to give birth in the street.

## What was done

Semmelweis eliminated hypotheses systematically. The clinics differed in staff, and doctors and
students came to the wards **directly from performing autopsies**. Midwives did not.

The decisive evidence came from a death: his colleague Jakob Kolletschka was cut by a student's
scalpel during an autopsy and died with the same pathology as the fever victims. Semmelweis inferred
that some "cadaverous particles" were being carried from the dissecting room to the patients.

He instituted handwashing in chlorinated lime. Mortality in the First Clinic fell sharply — to
roughly the level of the Second.

He was right, and by a mechanism nobody could yet explain: germ theory was two decades away.

## Why he failed anyway

**He published late and badly.** He resisted writing his findings up for years, and when he finally
did, the book was long, disorganised and polemical.

**He attacked his audience.** He wrote open letters calling established obstetricians irresponsible
and, in effect, murderers. Whatever the moral justice of that, it made adoption a confession of
guilt for everyone who had to adopt it.

**He had no mechanism, and offered no account of why the theory should be preferred** to the
prevailing miasma explanations, which also predicted that cleanliness helped.

**He did not control for the obvious objections** in a form his profession would accept as evidence.

He was dismissed, moved to Budapest, deteriorated, was committed to an asylum in 1865 and died there
within weeks, reportedly of an infection from a beating by guards.

## The principle

**Being right is a precondition, not an achievement.** A correct finding that is badly evidenced,
badly written, or delivered as an accusation will not be adopted — and the people it would have
helped are not saved by your having been right.

## For us

This is the case that most directly concerns Thejus's actual situation, and it cuts two ways.

**On the work.** Correctness is necessary and it is not sufficient. The BT3 history-planes bug and
the attention frame bug were real findings; what makes them *career assets* is that they were
written up clearly, corrected publicly, and framed without accusation
(`docs/writeup_attention_frame_bug.md`). The finding and the writing are one deliverable.

The same applies to Φ. If it passes F1, the result is worth nothing to anyone until it is stated in
a form a hiring panel can evaluate — with its honest limit attached, which is the part Semmelweis
would have omitted and which is the part that makes it credible.

**On how I deliver findings to Thejus.** Semmelweis's failure was tone as much as evidence. When I
find something wrong — the 693-game figure was wrong, the corpus is 9,000; the bullet composition
makes the profile measure reflex rather than knowledge; this leadership corpus tests non-negotiable
#6 — the useful version states the fact, shows the measurement, and proposes the next action. The
useless version is correct and unusable.

**And the reverse case, which is the one to fear.** Semmelweis was contradicted by the whole
profession and was right. Twice this week I reduced the severity of Gemini's findings after
measuring — 4e-08 instead of "precision loss", 0.0 instead of "parameter corruption". Those
measurements are in the record precisely so that if I turn out to be the establishment dismissing a
correct outsider, the evidence for my dismissal is auditable rather than merely asserted.
