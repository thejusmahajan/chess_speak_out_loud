# Sloan adjourning a meeting for lack of disagreement

**General Motors, 1920s–1950s. Alfred P. Sloan Jr. as president and chairman.**

**Provenance note:** the anecdote survives chiefly through Peter Drucker, who consulted at GM and
wrote it up in *The Effective Executive* (1967). It is Drucker's telling, not a minuted record.
Treat the wording as Drucker's and the practice as well attested.

## The situation

Sloan ran GM by committee, and committees have a characteristic disease: they converge. Agreement
arrives early, feels like progress, and is usually a sign that the alternatives have not been
examined rather than that they have been rejected.

## What was done

At a meeting of senior executives, on reaching quick and complete agreement on an important
decision, Sloan is reported to have said approximately:

> "Gentlemen, I take it we are all in complete agreement on the decision here… Then I propose we
> postpone further discussion of this matter until our next meeting, to give ourselves time to
> develop disagreement and perhaps gain some understanding of what the decision is all about."

The operative phrase is **"develop disagreement"** — disagreement as something you manufacture on
purpose, not something you tolerate when it happens to appear.

## Why it worked

Because unanimity is evidence about the *process*, not about the decision. If a hard question
produces instant consensus, the most likely explanations are that the group shares an unexamined
assumption, or that the alternatives were never generated, or that people are reading the room. None
of those is "we are right".

Drucker's broader argument is that the effective decision starts from conflicting views rather than
from a consensus to be defended, because the alternatives are the only protection against being
confidently wrong. A decision taken without a real alternative is not a decision; it is a
ratification.

## The principle

**Unanimity on a hard question is a warning. Manufacture the opposing case before committing, and
if nobody can construct one, postpone rather than proceed.**

## For us

This is the principle I misapplied so badly that it is the reason this corpus exists.

On 2026-09-01, Thejus proposed steering toward Lichess puzzle configurations. I wrote a round table
with four constructed voices, three of which argued against him, and landed on three amendments to
his idea. He rejected it and had it deleted from disk. The 2026-09-01 post-mortem identifies the
error precisely: I applied a device built for **auditing my own work** to **adjudicating his aim**.

Sloan's practice does not license that. Note who is manufacturing disagreement about what: Sloan is
the decision-maker, generating opposition to the proposal *he is about to adopt*. He is not
convening a panel to argue against a subordinate's proposal, and he is certainly not doing it to his
own principal.

The corrected form is now written into `discussions/README.md`: a round table is for auditing the
leader's work or an engineering choice, and it is convened when Thejus asks for one. When he did ask
— the training-optimisation round table of 2026-09-02 — the same device produced seven usable
agreements and one honest non-convergence.

**Where I should be using it and am not:** on my own conclusions when they arrive too easily. "The
steering target is `s_err`" was reached in a single pass and has been treated as settled ever since.
Nobody has constructed the case for `s_tac`. That is precisely the shape Sloan would have adjourned.
