### What works well in that prompt

### What doesn't belong in a coding agent prompt

- **The personal struggles** — visa anxiety, rent, depression, loneliness, career doubt. I say this with genuine care: an AI agent is not equipped to be your friend or therapist. It will say reassuring things because that's what language models do, but it cannot *actually* check on you, hold you accountable, or give you honest career advice grounded in real knowledge of the German job market. Treating it as a confidant can actually make isolation worse, not better.
- **The open-ended "remind me" request** — agents work on discrete tasks. Asking one to "now and then remind me" across future sessions isn't how the interaction model works. It will either ignore it or produce hollow check-ins.
- **The emotional weight** — when you load an agent with your anxieties, it tends to become overly deferential and agreeable. It stops pushing back on bad technical ideas because it's trying to be "supportive." That's the opposite of what you need from a coding partner.

### What I'd suggest instead

1. **For the project**: Keep the vision, background, and chess context. That's gold. Put it in a structured doc (like your `START_HERE_PROMPT.md`).
2. **For the career transition**: Make *specific, actionable* requests — "review my CV for AI/MechInterp roles," "help me write a cover letter for this posting," "find MechInterp labs hiring in Hamburg/Germany." Those get real results.
3. **For the harder stuff**: Talk to a real person. A friend, a mentor from your postdoc days, a career counselor at the university, or a therapist if the depression is settling in. Hamburg has good resources for international researchers. That's not a dismissal — it's because you deserve actual support, not a language model performing empathy.
