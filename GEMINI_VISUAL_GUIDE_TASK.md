## 0. The rules of engagement — read these twice

**Rule 1 — You may not do chess analysis. Ever.**
Do not evaluate a position, suggest a move, judge whether a move is good, or explain *why* a
chess move works, using your own knowledge. LC0 and Stockfish are the only sources of chess
truth in this project. Every chess claim in your output must be a restatement of something in
`docs/design_session/visual_guide/KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md`. If you need a chess fact that is not
there, **stop and ask.** Do not reason your way to it.

**Rule 2 — You may not invent a number.**
Not as a placeholder, not as an illustration, not "for now". Every digit that reaches the page
must be read from a JSON file at generation time. This project has already had a worker deliver
fabricated content that looked entirely plausible, and the whole verification apparatus below
exists because of it. A wrong number inside a beautiful diagram is the worst failure mode this
document has, because a picture is more convincing than a sentence.

**Rule 3 — The previous version of this document is wrong, and instructively so.**
`docs/design_session/neural_mcts_visual_guide.tex` describes a chess position that does not
exist, with priors that were never measured, and builds a whole chapter around a move (`e6+`)
that **is not legal in the position it claims to analyse**. It reads beautifully. That is the
point. Read `KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §0 before you write anything.

**Rule 4 — When you are uncertain, say so in the deliverable, in the right tier.**
The document has a four-tier honesty apparatus (measured / established / published research /
our hypothesis). Using it correctly is part of the job, not a caveat on it. A figure that
honestly says "we have not measured this" is worth more than one that quietly implies we have.

**Rule 5 — Write code, not content.**
Every figure with a number on it is *generated by a script from JSON*. You are writing a
generator and a layout, not typing numbers. If you find yourself hand-copying a value from a
table into a `.tex` file, you are doing the task wrong.

## 1. What you are building, and why

The user is a strong club player (~2100–2200 Lichess) who **works by visualisation**. He read the
existing guide and the verdict was simple: it is a wall of text *about* a search tree.

> "I want to see the variation trees as trees themselves. Then each step is explained by changing
> that picture little by little — so the guide is a pictorial representation of what is going on
> in the LC0 analysis, not just text."

So: **one canvas per idea, drawn once at fixed coordinates, then mutated one small change at a
time, with a one-line caption naming the change.** Eight small frames beat three clever ones.

The heart of the document is a real, measured, eight-iteration LC0 search on a four-legal-move
king-and-pawn endgame — small enough that **every node in the tree can be drawn**, not
schematised. You will animate it frame by frame: nodes appearing, edges thickening, a gold path
walking down and a green value walking back up, and a strip of stacked bars underneath in which
*the tallest bar is the move the search picks* — which is the entire PUCT formula, seen rather
than explained.

## 3. The work, in five phases

Deliver a short checkpoint note at the end of each phase (a few lines, in the report file). Do
not silently proceed past a failing gate.

### Phase 1 — Verify the ground before building on it *(do not skip)*

1. `cd docs/design_session/visual_guide/tikz && pdflatex -interaction=nonstopmode _probe.tex`
   → must exit 0. Render it to PNG and **look at it**; this shows you the visual grammar.
2. `cd docs/design_session/book && python tools/simulate_search.py`
   → must print `OK` on every line and no `MISMATCH` in the cross-check.
3. Open `engine_data.json` and confirm for yourself the three headline tables in
   `KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md` §2.1, §2.4 and §3. **You are checking my work.** If any number in the
   knowledge base disagrees with the JSON, stop and report it — do not "fix" it silently and do
   not proceed.

**Checkpoint 1:** report the three results. If anything failed, stop here.

### Phase 2 — The generator

Write `docs/design_session/visual_guide/tools/make_figures.py` per `LATEX_SPEC.md` §5.

- Reads the JSON + imports `simulate()` from `simulate_search.py`.
- Re-runs the engine cross-check at `nodes=8` **before writing anything**; on mismatch, prints it
  and exits non-zero having written no files.
- Emits `figures/fig_*.tex` (one `tikzpicture` per frame) and `tools/figure_data.json`
  (every number, with the JSON path it came from).
- No numeric literal anywhere in the script except layout constants.

**Checkpoint 2:** paste the provenance report for FIG-2.2 — every printed digit and its source
path.

### Phase 3 — PART 2, the growing tree *(this is the document)*

Build FIG-2.0 → FIG-2.11 exactly as storyboarded, on the fixed canvas coordinates in
`LATEX_SPEC.md` §4. Spend the majority of your effort here.

The three frames that carry the most weight — get these right before anything else:

- **FIG-2.2 (iteration 2).** Kd6 has just returned the best measured value in the position and
  the search does **not** pick it again, because its `U` halved the instant it was visited. If a
  reader takes one frame away from this document, it is this one.
- **FIG-2.3 (iteration 3).** The first time selection does not stop at the root — it recurses,
  and the second row of the tree appears.
- **FIG-2.10 (the sign flip).** `+0.95129` at the leaf becomes `−0.95129` at the Black-to-move
  edge and `+0.95129` again at the root edge. Draw the flip; do not narrate it. Getting a sign
  backwards here would make every arrow in the document a lie.

**Checkpoint 3:** compile, render PART 2 to PNG, and report. Include the output of
`diff figures/fig_2_3.tex figures/fig_2_4.tex` — it should show only the intended changes.

### Phase 4 — PARTS 3, 0, 1, 4, 6

In that order (see `VISUAL_PLAN.md` §6 for why). PART 4's Morphy data is spectacular and almost
free: at 400 nodes the engine plays Qb7 with a forced mate in two sitting at **zero visits and
1.60% policy**; by 1600 nodes it has found it, given it three visits, scored it 1.000, and
changed its mind — then four times more thinking changes nothing at all.

### Phase 5 — PART 5, and the honesty pass

PART 5 is the softest ground in the document and the easiest place to accidentally lie. Every
figure there is either a labelled cartoon or inside a purple `hypothesis` box. **Delete v1's
invented probe numbers** ("Layer 8 internally calculates a 95% winning attack") rather than
reproducing them.

Then a final pass over the whole document: every figure inside its correct tier box, every
number traceable, nothing from the `e6+` fiction surviving in any caption.

## 5. Definition of done

- `python tools/make_figures.py` exits 0, self-check against the engine passes.
- `pdflatex` (twice) exits 0.
- Every page has been rendered to PNG and inspected. **A figure that compiles is not a figure
  that reads** — overlapping labels, collided arrows and clipped boards all compile perfectly
  happily, and the leader will be looking at exactly this.
- Every printed digit traces to a JSON path in `figure_data.json`.
- Within a figure family, shared elements sit at byte-identical coordinates; at most two things
  change per frame; nothing slides or re-scales.
- No colour appears outside `\vglegend`.
- No chess claim anywhere that is not in `KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md`.

## 6. When you get stuck

- **A number you need does not exist** → say so in `BUILD_REPORT.md`, draw the figure without it
  or drop the figure, and move on. Do not estimate it.
- **The storyboard asks for something the macros cannot draw** → add a macro to `mctsviz.sty`,
  document it in the file's comments, re-run `_probe.tex`, and note it in the report.
- **The storyboard and the knowledge base disagree** → the knowledge base wins. Report the
  conflict.
- **A figure is too crowded** → split it into two frames. That is always the right answer here,
  and it is what the whole design is built around.
- **You think the plan is wrong somewhere** → say so plainly in the report, with your reasoning,
  and then build the plan as specified unless it would require you to print something false.
  Judgement calls about layout are yours; judgement calls about content are not.
