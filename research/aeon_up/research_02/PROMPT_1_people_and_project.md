I have an interview for a postdoctoral position at Helmholtz-Zentrum Hereon, Institute of Coastal
Environmental Chemistry, in Geesthacht, Germany. The project is called AEON-UP — probabilistic deep
learning for urban air quality. The two principal investigators are **Dr. Matthias Karl** and
**Dr. Martin Ramacher**, both at that institute. I need to know what they have actually published
and whether the project exists publicly.

Find, with sources:

**1. Dr. Matthias Karl's papers on ultrafine particles (UFP) or particle number concentration
(PNC).** For each one: the exact title, journal, year, volume, page range, DOI, and the full author
list with Karl's position in it. Take these from the publisher's page, the DOI resolver, or
Hereon's own publication database — not from a Google Scholar or ResearchGate rendering, which
often carry mangled titles. **List every UFP paper you find; do not choose one for me.** To confirm
you have the right person, cross-check against this citation, which I have already verified:
Karl et al. (2019), *Geoscientific Model Development* 12, 3357–3389, doi:10.5194/gmd-12-3357-2019,
the EPISODE-CityChem Part 2 Hamburg application. I may name one of these papers to its own author,
so the title has to be exact.

**2. Everything either PI has published or preprinted since 1 January 2024.** Title, venue, year,
DOI, and one sentence quoted from the abstract so I can tell what it is. Treat the two separately.
**Flag any paper involving machine learning, statistical emulation, data assimilation, or
uncertainty quantification.** My understanding is that Karl has no published machine-learning
record; if that has changed, it is the single most useful thing you can tell me.

**3. Does AEON-UP exist publicly as a named, funded project?** A project page, a grant record, or a
funding-database entry. If it does: the funder and programme (BMBF, DFG, EU/Horizon, Helmholtz
internal), the partners, and the project period. What the acronym expands to **only if a source
states it** — do not assemble an expansion from the letters; a guessed expansion said aloud in the
interview is hard to recover from. If nothing public exists, one line saying so is a complete and
useful answer.

**4. Any new software release, consortium, or project** either of them is publicly associated with
since 2024.

**Rules.** Every fact comes back with a URL and the exact sentence you read from it, in quotation
marks — not a paraphrase. If you cannot confirm something from an authoritative source, write
**UNVERIFIED** and move on: a gap costs me nothing, because I simply will not raise that subject,
whereas a confident guess gets said out loud to the person who wrote the paper. Do not soften an
unknown with "likely", "appears to be", or "approximately". Do not give me advice about my
application — it has already been submitted and cannot change. Do not summarise Hereon as an
institution or characterise what these two researchers are interested in. List what they published.
